Atlas · Personal Internet Presence

Your devices.Your rules.Your presence.

Atlas gives you a Personal Internet Presence: one identity and one set of rules that follow you across your phone, laptop and computer, on any Wi-Fi or carrier. You decide where each site or app sees you connecting from, and your rules are locked on your own devices, where Atlas cannot read them.

Already have a VPN?

A VPN changes your address. Atlas protects who you are.

A VPN app sends your traffic through its servers so sites see a different address. That helps, but it stops there. The VPN company still holds your account, you are still the same person to every site you sign in to, and what gets kept about you is up to that company's policy.

Atlas is built around you instead of around a server. Here is what it protects:

Services can't link you together

Each service that accepts Atlas sees its own identifier for you, so two services cannot compare notes and work out that they share the same person.

No company holds your account

Your account comes from a recovery phrase only you hold, plus a recovery code you write down. Atlas keeps no copy and has no override, so nobody can take it over through us.

Your rules stay unreadable

Your rules are encrypted on your own devices before they reach Atlas. Atlas stores them, but cannot read which sites you route or where.

No trail of where you went

Atlas does not log the sites your traffic reaches, and the exits you connect through keep no record of which account used which address.

No silent leaks

If Atlas cannot confirm your connection leaves where you chose, it stops, restores your ordinary connection and tells you, instead of quietly sending traffic somewhere else.

How it works

Three steps, then it stays out of your way.

  1. 1

    Install Atlas on your devices

    Start on one device, then add the others with your key. Each device gets its own key under the same identity, so losing a laptop never changes who you are to the services you use.

  2. 2

    Set your rules

    Pick your everyday city, then add rules for the sites and apps that should appear from somewhere else, or stay on your own connection.

  3. 3

    Atlas keeps your presence steady

    As you move between home Wi-Fi, cellular and hotel networks, Atlas keeps your rules in place and checks that each connection really leaves where you chose.

Privacy & data

Built so Atlas is not the weak point.

Atlas keeps no record of the sites you visit, does not join your account to the network addresses you use, and cannot read your rules. Those are design choices, not a policy that can be quietly revised.

Where Atlas is today

Built, tested, and nearly open.

  1. 1

    We published the question first

    Done

    Before writing the apps, Atlas Papers 001 and 002 asked publicly whether the Internet was missing a person-controlled identity layer, and invited networking and security experts to say we were wrong.

    Read the papers →
  2. 2

    We built the identity engine

    Built

    An identity rooted in a phrase only you hold, a published verification key, and a different, unlinkable identifier for every service. Running code, not a mockup.

  3. 3

    We proved it end to end

    Verified

    A demonstration service verifies an Atlas identity from the published key alone, and the exits your devices connect through keep no record of which account used them.

    Read the trust paper →
  4. 4

    Invited testers are using it

    Happening now

    The Mac, iPhone, Windows and Android apps are in the hands of invited testers. It is the same system described here, not a separate demo.

  5. 5

    What happens before public sign-up

    Not yet

    One step remains: an outside-reviewed cryptographic change (blind signatures) that turns today's no-storage rule into a mathematical guarantee. Testing stays invite-only until it ships.
