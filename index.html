You

You at the centre, not the companies around you.

The network changes. You don't.

Home Wi-Fi, your phone's data, a hotel or a café: every network you join is different. With Atlas, your account, your choices and where sites see you connecting from stay the same on all of them.

What you get

Your device is personal. Make your connection more private.

At home, on mobile data or on public Wi-Fi, the network carrying your connection can learn about the sites you visit, and that information is valuable to the companies that collect it. Privacy includes having a say in what your connection reveals.

Keep your browsing to yourself

Layer9i keeps no record of the sites you visit, so there is nothing to sell and nothing to hand over.

Choose where you connect from

With Atlas, choose where you connect from for each site. Use a location in your home country for banking, or choose another location for browsing.

Available locations: Toronto, Montreal, New York, London, and Amsterdam.

More than one way to connect

Some sites work better on your own connection. Others can go through a trusted Atlas location. Atlas lets you use both, without changing the connection for everything else.

Keep control across your devices

Manage your choices in one app, on Mac, iPhone, Windows and Android. Your saved choices are encrypted on your device, so they stay private, even from Layer9i.

Your account belongs to you

It comes from a key only you hold. Layer9i can't take it over or hand it to anyone. The flip side: keep your key and recovery code safe, because Layer9i can't get them back for you.

Know when something's wrong

Atlas checks that your connection goes where you chose. If it can't confirm that, it's built to switch itself off, put your normal connection back and tell you.

Already have a VPN?

A VPN hides your address. Atlas privately puts you in control.

A VPN app sends your connection through its own servers, so sites see its address instead of yours. That's useful, but you're still trusting that company with your account and with whatever it decides to keep about you.

Atlas starts from a different place:

You hold the keys

Your account comes from a phrase only you have. Layer9i keeps no copy, so there's nothing for anyone to take.

Your choices stay private

Which sites you send where is locked on your devices. Layer9i stores only the locked copy and has no way to open it.

No record of where you go

Layer9i doesn't log the sites you visit, and the servers you connect through keep no record of which account used them.

Coming next: signing in to websites with Atlas, where each site gets a different ID for you so sites can't compare notes about you. It already works on Layer9i's test site. Real websites come next.

How it works

Three steps, then get on with your day.

  1. 1

    Install Atlas and save your key

    Your account is a private key that only your devices hold. Start on one device, then add the others from Devices, which shows your key: keep a copy somewhere safe, along with the one-time recovery code Atlas gives you. Lose a laptop and your account isn't lost with it: your key brings it back.

  2. 2

    Set your Rules

    Pick your Default City: where websites and apps see you from. Then, in Rules, add exceptions, such as your bank going through a trusted city in your own country, or local sites using your own connection.

  3. 3

    Go about your life

    As you move between home Wi-Fi, your phone's data and hotel networks, Atlas keeps your Rules in place and checks that you really appear where you chose (Intended vs. Observed on the Home screen). If it can't confirm that, it turns itself off and tells you, so your connection is never quietly wrong.

Privacy & data

Your privacy, built in.

With Atlas, the sites you visit stay yours. Layer9i keeps no record of them, keeps your account separate from the internet addresses you use, and encrypts your choices so only you can read them.

This privacy is part of how Atlas works, not a line in a policy.
