Atlas Identity — the one-pager

One identity a person holds themselves, trusted by every site, carried on a network Layer9i owns. Private by design, and already running.

In one line

Atlas Identity lets people be private and trusted at the same time: one identity they hold, recognised by every site, with nothing to track or sell.

Built and running

  • Apps on four platforms: iPhone and iPad, Mac, Android and Windows, all on one identity.
  • Five cities live: Toronto, Montreal, New York, London and Amsterdam.
  • Layer9i's own internet network: a registered network number and address blocks, with signed routes, mapped city by city for location services.
  • An identity the person holds: created on their own phone from a recovery phrase only they have, approving each of their devices.
  • A separate ID for every site: a demonstration bank already signs people in with Atlas.
  • Live subscriptions: sign-up, renewal and cancellation run end to end. Free while invite-only; $7.99 a month or $69.99 a year after.
  • Security checked in public: every server tested, account isolation proven on the live network, 7 of 7 issues fixed and published.
The ask

$400k to $750k to take Atlas from invite-only to public launch.

  • Instrument: post-money SAFE, $6M to $8M cap
  • Launch: independent security test and app store releases
  • Network: every city on Layer9i addresses, then four more cities
  • Product: ad and tracker blocking, family plans and the home unit
  • Runway: founder runway and first hires through launch

Exact amount and cap are set before any signed note.

Business model

A subscription: $7.99 a month or $69.99 a year, every device included, no ads and no data sales. Next, family and small-team plans and the home unit. Later, services that sign people in with Atlas pay for the trust it gives them.

Next

  • Every city on Layer9i's own addresses (New York is already; the others follow as location services catch up).
  • Ad and tracker blocking.
  • An independent penetration test, then app store releases and public sign-up.
  • The Atlas home unit for TVs and home devices, and four more cities: Vancouver, Chicago, Los Angeles and Frankfurt.
