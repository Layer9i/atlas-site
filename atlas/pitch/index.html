What Atlas is

Your connection changes.Your presence doesn't.

Atlas Identity starts with the person, not the network, the device or the app. It gives you one identity that you hold, that follows you across Wi-Fi, mobile data and every device, and that sites can trust without anyone watching what you do.

The problem

The internet trusts your connection, not you.

Sites decide whether to trust you from where your connection comes from. Change networks, travel or try to stay private, and your bank, your streaming and your work treat you like a stranger: extra checks, blocked logins, locked accounts.

And the tools meant to protect your privacy often make it worse. They send everyone out through the same shared addresses, so sites can't tell people apart, and the companies in the middle can see where you go.

What Atlas does

Trusted, private and in control. At the same time.

Trusted. Your bank, your work and your accounts see one consistent you, from a network that Layer9i owns and answers for.

Private. No record of the sites you visit. Your key and your rules stay locked on your own devices, and every site gets its own separate ID, so they can't compare notes about you.

In control. Choose the city each part of your life appears from, see exactly what a site can tell about you, and get warned when an app checks in somewhere unknown.

Our own network

An identity shouldn't have to borrow someone else's address.

Most privacy tools rent the same cloud addresses that sites already distrust. Layer9i is a registered network operator with its own network number and addresses, signed and mapped city by city, so the people behind them aren't lumped in with strangers.

Live today in Toronto, Montreal, New York, London and Amsterdam, with Vancouver, Chicago, Los Angeles and Frankfurt already reserved.

Day to day

Your connection changed. You didn't.

Go from home Wi-Fi to mobile data, to a hotel, to the office. Atlas keeps you sealed, keeps you appearing from the places you chose, and checks that sites really see what you asked for. If it ever can't, it tells you and steps aside rather than leaving you unprotected without knowing.
